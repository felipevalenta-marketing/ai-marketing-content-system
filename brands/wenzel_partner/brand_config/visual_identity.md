# Visual Identity

## Visual Direction
The visual language should feel editorial, architectural, warm, and understated.
Luxury is expressed through restraint, proportion, and quality rather than excess.
The brand should feel premium but approachable, with clear Mallorca context.

## Palette Direction
Preferred mood:
- ivory
- warm white
- charcoal
- black
- champagne
- stone

Accent options:
- deep green
- muted gold
- midnight blue
- sand
- terracotta
- sea blue

## Typography Direction
- Use elegant, high-contrast serif type for headlines where appropriate.
- Pair with a clean sans-serif for supporting text and UI elements.
- Keep spacing generous and alignment intentional.

## Photography Direction
Images should feel:
- bright but controlled
- cinematic
- spacious
- natural
- premium
- grounded in Mallorca
- lifestyle-oriented
- locally authentic

Preferred subjects:
- interiors with strong natural light
- architectural details
- lifestyle moments with restraint
- exterior shots with clean composition
- family homes
- renovation potential
- terraces, gardens, and outdoor living
- neighborhoods and local context
- premium properties, but not only premium properties

Avoid:
- overly saturated colors
- cluttered scenes
- stock-photo stiffness
- visible gimmicks or trend-chasing effects
- imagery that feels only aspirational and disconnected from real life
- visuals that imply the brand serves only an ultra-luxury audience

## Layout Principles
- Use whitespace as a luxury signal.
- Keep composition balanced and calm.
- Favor vertical hierarchy and clear focal points.
- Let one strong visual lead instead of crowding the frame.
- Keep information easy to scan for buyers, sellers, and relocation clients.
- Use visual systems that can flex between luxury listings and practical market guidance.

## Multi-Brand Rule
Visual identity tokens should be customizable per brand.
The shared engine should only assume structure, not color or typography constants.
